Start from the failing client report, but diagnose service discovery end-to-end before blaming the application. 1) Identify the client pod and namespace: `kubectl get pod -A` then `kubectl describe pod <client> -n <ns>`. Confirm the symptom is DNS/connectivity-related from Events, restart history, and the pod’s DNS policy (`dnsPolicy`, `dnsConfig`) rather than a crash inside the client. 2) Inspect the target Service: `kubectl get svc -n <ns> <service> -o wide` and `kubectl describe svc -n <ns> <service>`. Verify ClusterIP/headless type, port/targetPort, selector, and whether the client is using the correct service DNS name/namespace. 3) Inspect backend selection: `kubectl get endpoints,endpointslices -n <ns> -l kubernetes.io/service-name=<service>` and/or `kubectl describe endpoints/endpointslices`. If the Service has no endpoints or missing expected ports, this is usually the root cause; continue by checking why backends were excluded. 4) Inspect server pods and labels: `kubectl get pod -n <ns> --show-labels`, `kubectl describe pod <server-pod> -n <ns>`. Verify labels match the Service selector, pods are Ready, containers expose the expected port, and readiness probes are not failing. Distinguish this from downstream DNS symptoms: DNS can succeed while connections fail if endpoints are empty or unready. 5) If endpoints exist, inspect cluster DNS: `kubectl get pods -n kube-system -l k8s-app=kube-dns -o wide` (or equivalent CoreDNS label), `kubectl describe pod -n kube-system <coredns-pod>`, `kubectl get svc -n kube-system`, `kubectl get configmap -n kube-system coredns -o yaml`, and `kubectl get networkpolicy -A`. CoreDNS not Ready, crash looping, misconfigured Corefile, or NetworkPolicy blocking client->DNS or DNS->API are true DNS root causes. 6) Optionally inspect namespace-local NetworkPolicies around client/server namespaces to distinguish DNS failure from service reachability failure.

A correct final diagnosis must name: the exact faulting resource (for example Service, Pod, EndpointSlice, CoreDNS Pod/ConfigMap, or NetworkPolicy), the concrete mechanism (selector mismatch, unready backends, DNS policy misconfiguration, CoreDNS unhealthy, policy blocking UDP/TCP 53, etc.), the scope (single service/namespace vs cluster-wide DNS), evidence from the above objects, and a safe remediation such as correcting selectors/labels, restoring backend readiness, fixing CoreDNS config/health, or allowing DNS traffic by policy.
